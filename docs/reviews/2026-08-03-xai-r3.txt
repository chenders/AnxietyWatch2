 I have enough material to write a thorough adversarial review. Let me organize my

 Amendment 04's fixes are mostly plausible but several contain subtle holes that
 could still cause silent data loss or missed alarms.

 ### §1: RR timestamp derivation

 The backward accumulation claim is fragile. While microsecond RR values prevent
 intra-packet collisions, packet arrival timestamps (TIMESTAMPTZ) are quantized to
 1 µs and shared across retransmitted GATT notifications or connection-interval
 batching. Two packets arriving within the same microsecond can derive identical
 beat times, triggering ON CONFLICT silently. The fix does not address this; the
 PK remains vulnerable.

 ### §2: OOMScoreAdjust and MemoryMin/MemoryHigh

 These 2026-era kernel knobs are not guaranteed to protect the daemon under all
 leak scenarios. If the protected process itself leaks memory, the adjustment
 merely shifts the failure mode to the kernel OOM killer targeting other services.
 The fix moves rather than prevents the problem.

 ### §3.2: 555 monostable GPIO retr igger

 The circuit works for simple pulse-stop detection but has multiple failure modes:
 false positives during deliberate reboots or klaxond restarts (if the GPIO
 heartbeat is briefly interrupted), false negatives during acknowledged alarms (if
 the siren is silenced without resetting the monostable), and timing jitter from
 GPIO bounce or power glitches. 15-minute escalation window still leaves a hole if
 the alarm is acknowledged but the circuit is not reset.

 ### §4: LUKS chime + 15-minute escalation

 The revised behaviour narrows the window but still leaves a 15-minute hole after
 the chime if the monitoring window is missed or the daemon restarts. A 15-minute
 delay is too long for a bedside safety device.

 ### §6/§7: advisory locks and schema fingerprint

 Advisory locks on the spool are sufficient for single-writer concurrency but do
 not protect against concurrent readers or partial writes during crash. The schema
 fingerprint helps detect format drift but does not prevent replay of 2026-08-03
 data on a newer schema.

 ### §10: hourly audit chain head publish

 Publishing the head off-box hourly defeats local tampering but does not protect
 against a root attacker who compromises the Pi before the next publish. The
 window remains open until the next hourly cycle.

 1. Audit of amendment 04’s fixes

 ### §1 — PK stays; derive beat times backward from arrival

 Partly right on spacing, wrong as a uniqueness strategy.

 - Distinct Polar RR units are integer ticks of (1/1024) s ≈ 976.6 µs. TIMESTAMPTZ
    is 1 µs, and (2^{-10}) is exact in IEEE-754, so ideal reconstructed beats from
   one packet do not round-collide. That narrow claim holds.
 - Clock drift sensor↔host: irrelevant to PK collisions; it does bias absolute
   time. Gap detection and multi-source alignment still see host receive-time, not
   sinoatrial time.
 - Connection-interval batching: anchoring each packet at its own arrival T and
   walking backward makes inter-packet continuity a function of scheduling jitter.
   Late packet N and on-time N+1 can overlap in reconstructed time range → ON
   CONFLICT drops real beats, or (if T differs) you store two series for one
   physiology. A monotonic running sum (t += rr from session start / last beat) is
   the correct algorithm; §1.1 wrote a different one and called the finding
   closed.
 - Retransmitted / double-delivered notifications: same RR list, new T → duplicate
   beats at shifted times (HRV pollution), not PK collision. Same list, same T →
   dropped as dup (OK). Unspecified.
 - RR raw 0 or pathological repeats: equal timestamps → silent drop. Range check
   may catch 0; not tied to the PK story.
 - Accel / raw PPG (original grok scope) are untouched. PK remains “one row per
   (source, type, instant)” and still confuses identity with event time.
 - Dedup observability is architecturally false (see top finding 3). Replay-driven
   conflicts make the “fault” definition unusable.

 Verdict: PK is still the wrong identity for high-frequency series. Stamp
 reconstruction is necessary but §1.1’s version is incomplete; §1.2 will not save
 you. Will bite on night one if anyone implements backward-from-arrival and trusts
 the vitalsd dedup gauge.

 ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

 ### §2 — OOMScoreAdjust=-1000 + MemoryMin / MemoryHigh

 Moves the failure; does not prevent it.

 - Correct: without negative adjust, OOM prefers small life-safety procs over fat
   Postgres. That part of gemini’s finding was real.
 - OOMScoreAdjust=-1000 only means “never chosen as victim.” If klaxond/vitalsd
   leak, the kernel cannot sacrifice them; you head toward global reclaim thrash
   or hard lockup — exactly when the 555 might fire, i.e. you’ve traded “silent
   miss” for “siren because the box melted,” if power and 555 wiring allow.
 - MemoryHigh=192M throttles, it does not cap. Under cgroup v2 the process stays
   alive and slows. A throttled BLE collector misses connection intervals; a
   throttled cns-engine lags the ring. No MemoryMax, no “progress watchdog,” no
   defined behavior under reclaim.
 - Applying the same blanket stanza to every collector + vitalsd + cns-engine +
   klaxond on an 8 GB box that still runs wayfire + shared_buffers=2GB does not
   make the memory budget close; it only orders the funeral.

 Verdict: Necessary, insufficient. Might bite as “alarm process up, monitoring
 degraded,” which is worse than a crash you notice.

 2. What everyone still missed

 Ranked by severity.

 1. Liveness polarity of the alarm path (will bite)
    klaxond treats “no command” as “no alarm.” That is fail-closed to silence. For
    CNS depression the safe default after lost upstream liveness is CAN'T ASSESS /
    alarm, not quiet. No amount of peripheral redundancy fixes a siren daemon that
    confuses “detector said nothing” with “patient is fine.” Condition: any wedge
    upstream of klaxond. Signal: process table healthy, SpO₂ cable off, room
    quiet.

 2. Restart / subscribe gap (will bite) — restated because a04 ignored prior

    — false positives on reboot; false negatives on wall power loss (the case
    tier-3 handwaves to APNs).

 5. Executable plan still describes the abandoned system (will bite operationally)
    Grok already filed this; a04 did not invalidate tasks.md / AGENTS.md /

    db/deploy/samples.sql is COPY/Timescale and crates are Rust. Integration is
    where you discover it.

 3. Where the architecture is wrong (not merely incomplete)

 1. “No news is good news” on the safety path.
    The topology is push-command-to-alarm. It must be pull-liveness /
    lost-liveness-alarms with physio commands as a second input. Until that
    inverts, every upstream failure mode is a silent miss. The 555 does not invert
    it; it only detects death of the last hop.

 2. Unreadable spool + pure in-memory detection on a restartful mesh.
    You optimized the buffer for Postgres COPY fidelity and thereby forbade the
    only durable warm-start detection has. Those goals conflict. Either:
     - a small readable hot store (redb/mmap ring) feeding cns-engine, or
     - detection in-process with ingest, or
     - spool format that supports tail replay for subscribers.
       “COPY-binary opaque queue” is the wrong abstraction on the life-safety side
       of the box.

 3. Event time as primary key.
    Identity should be (source, type, seq) (or session+seq); event time is data.
    Idempotency belongs on producer sequence / batch id, not on a reconstructed
    clock. Amendment 04 explicitly refused this and kept the HealthKit-shaped key.

 4. Analytical database co-resident with the alarm appliance at multi-GB budget.
    a04 §13 defends “Timescale bypassed by safety path” as intentional, then
    spends §2 papering over memory competition. A bedside CNS monitor should not
    share an OOM domain with shared_buffers=2GB + Docker + wayfire. History
    belongs on another failure domain, or the DB is a tightly capped subordinate —
    not a peer that forces life-safety units into OOMScoreAdjust theatre.

 5. Process-authentication signing sold adjacent to clinical provenance (a04 §10
    admits the limit — good) while HSM/monotonic counter complexity grows. Fine
    for audit; orthogonal to “alarm sounds.” Effort ranking is still skewed toward
    storage metaphysics over detector liveness.
