 1. What is missing that will hurt

 ### Will bite

 A. No unique key that fits high-frequency samples
 Severity: critical.
 RR, accel, and raw PPG cannot use (source, type, timestamp) unless timestamp is a
 synthetic monotonic sequence (cumulative RR sum, sample index) with
 sub-millisecond identity — and then it is not “event time” for gapfill/retention.
 The schema gives you one row per source/type/instant and calls collisions
 idempotency. For Polar that is data loss; for BP cuff it is mostly fine.
 How you know: spike log line count ≫ samples rows; samples_1min.sample_count caps
 near 60 for RR when it should be ~HR.

 B. Life-safety consume path is underspecified relative to the spool
 Severity: critical.
 Amendment §2: spool is unreadable except by the drainer; cns-engine keeps ~60 s
 rings. So detection must be fed by vitals_subscribe fanout from vitalsd, not from
 Postgres. Missing:

 - Order of operations: fanout before/after spool fsync? Ack to collector when?
 - Subscriber backpressure: slow cns-engine → full socket buffer → drop from
   detection while spool still durable (or block collectors).
 - vitalsd restart: rings empty; no warm replay from spool (by design unreadable);
   detection blind for the restart gap.
 - Postgres down is fine for ingest; vitalsd down is not fine for alarm, and the
   topology diagram still draws the alarm path through it.

 How you know: journal shows reconnect storms while samples is complete; tier
 machine silent across a vitalsd restart during a real desat.

 C. Off-box durability is deferred past the point data matters
 Severity: high.
 T-C2: tier 2 deferred; repo on same NVMe. Design §4.2 claimed three independently
 sufficient tiers. Today you have one disk with two directories. Stage 3 begins
 writing “samples worth keeping” under G3 after restore verify — restore from the
 same failed device is theatre.
 How you know: only when the NVMe dies.

 D. Acoustic alarm is not actually tested
 Severity: high (life-safety).
 Design §5.1 self-test = ALSA write accounting. That tests the digital path into
 the USB gadget, not pressure waves in the room. Missing: disable USB autosuspend
 on the DAC, hard volume floor, and a real proof (second device, mic loopback,
 load sense — pick one).
 How you know: morning self-test green, night silent.

 E. Drain liveness is not monitored
 Severity: high.
 vitals-spool-drain can be “active” and make no progress (PG in recovery, blocking
 lock, --verify mismatch after schema change). Spool grows until ENOSPC; then
 append behavior is unspecified. Health that only checks systemctl is-active lies.
 Need: monotonic drain offset / lag bytes / lag age on the health socket, and a
 degraded state that names the blocker (AGENTS.md already requires this pattern;
 the drain service is not under it).

 F. Timestamp authority for RR is undefined
 Severity: high for any HRV/CNS logic.
 Notification arrival time ≠ beat time. Multiple RRs in one packet need timestamps
 derived from a running clock of RR sums (or H10’s clock if exposed), not
 clock_gettime once per callback. Design never says who stamps
 vitals_sample.timestamp. Wrong stamps break gap detection, sleep staging, and any
 rate estimator.
 How you know: RR series jitter-clusters on packet boundaries; gaps anticorrelate
 with connection-interval batching.

 ### Might bite

 G. Late HealthKit / phone rows into compressed chunks
 Compression after 7 days (amendment §3.1) + phone push of backfill → inserts into
 compressed ranges (decompress/recompress, or failure under load). No policy for
 “reject older than N” vs “decompress.”

 H. Continuous aggregate lag as a footgun for anything near-real-time
 end_offset => 1 minute means samples_1min is knowingly stale. Fine if only charts
 use it; harmful if any scorer or silence detector shortcuts to the CA. Not
 forbidden in the docs.

 I. Registry reconcile vs historical semantics
 Rows ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE on service start (amendment §7.1). Changing range_hi
 does not re-quarantine old rows; renaming meaning of id is permanent. No
 type-version column. One careless TOML edit reinterprets forever.

 J. bluetoothd / adapter reset is outside the supervision tree
 Collectors restart; the daemon that owns the radio may not be coupled with hard
 deps and a known-good reset path. Multi-hour freezes with “collector up, zero
 notifications” are the common BlueZ failure mode. T-B1 measures the happy path
 once; it does not produce a recovery runbook.

 K. Restore verify is weaker than the design claims
 Design §4.2: row-count and checksum. T-C3: count(*) only. Equal counts with
 truncated values pass. Staleness >45 days is a fault on the panel — good — but
 the monthly check can green-wash corruption.

 L. Cold start of the whole box after power loss
 Watchdog reboots the board; ordered dependencies among bluetooth, postgresql,
 vitalsd, collectors, cns-engine, klaxond are not written as a single target with
 ConditionPathExists on the DAC and adapter. You can get “alarm up, radios down”
 or the reverse without a named degraded state at the system level.

 ### Taste (still real, lower)

 - No disk-watermark policy for spool/PG (what % free trips a phone push).
 - No explicit node_id backup/restore story once HLC is mostly decorative (see
   §3).
 - Stage deliverables still document the SQLite path; they will train you to
   mis-debug.

 3. Where the design is wrong, not just incomplete

 ### Hybrid Logical Clock — not justified for the system you are building

 Design §2.1: HLC is “exactly right for a Pi, a phone, and a watch that each
 observe reality independently.”

 After amendment 01, replication progress is a byte offset in a COPY spool, not an
 HLC cursor (amendment §2.2). The scar that justified HLC (“cursor = .now”) is
 fixed by offset-at-commit, not by HLC. Remaining writers:

 ┌────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────┐
 │ Writer         │ Key space                         │
 ├────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────┤
 │ BLE collectors │ Distinct source ids via vitalsd   │
 ├────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────┤
 │ Phone HK push  │ source=healthkit                  │
 ├────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────┤
 │ Spool replay   │ Same rows, ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING │
 └────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────┘

 Nobody concurrent-writes the same (source, type, timestamp) with different values
 as a merge problem. Phone vs Polar are different sources. Two nodes do not share
 a cuff.

 What HLC still does: decorate rows; invite a sync design you no longer have;
 complicate COPY encoding and indexes. What it does not do: fix the real collision
 problem (same source, same second, many RR). Using HLC as uniqueness would be
 closer to coherent; storing it while keying on wall time is the worst hybrid.

 Verdict: vestigial after the spool amendment. Keep node_id if you want
 provenance; drop HLC until a real multi-writer merge exists. Might bite as
 complexity; will not save you from RR loss.

 ### COPY-binary spool — right impulse, wrong confidence

 Dropping SQLite-as-translation-layer is correct (amendment §1). “Buffer format =
 Postgres format” is a real invariant.

 Where it is clever for its own sake:

 - You now own a non-portable, schema-frozen, dual-process queue with rotation,
   partial-segment trailers, and upgrade runbooks (amendment §2.3), i.e. a storage
   engine.
 - The common case is local PG on the same NVMe. A writer that COPY-streams live
   and only pages to disk on backpressure gives the same “PG down doesn’t block
   sensing” property without a second daemon in the steady state.
 - Idempotency via staging + ON CONFLICT is required because COPY cannot upsert —
   that is a tax on the format choice, paid forever.

 Not wrong enough to rip out if implemented with flock, atomic offsets, and lag
 metrics — but do not tell yourself it is simpler than SQLite. It is simpler than
 SQLite and Postgres. It is not simpler than Postgres alone + overflow file.

 ### Sqitch structure vs generated reference rows — sound, currently forked

 Amendment §7.1 split is right. db/deploy/registry_tables.sql is structure-only;
 good.

 Wrong in practice today:

 - T-D1 still tells the generator to emit structure + CHECKs into registry.sql.
 - Amendment admits the generator must be trimmed “as follow-up.”
 - Until that lands, two owners do claim the same objects.

 Also: FKs from samples to vitals_source/vitals_type mean drain fails hard if a
 collector builds against a newer registry than registry.sql reconcile on the
 server. That is good fail-closed behavior only if deploy order is registry
 reconcile before collector start — not written into systemd.

 ### One process per collector — right for C, inertia for Rust

 Design §3 / §5.1: process isolation because C shared state is toxic.

 c-vs-rust-evaluation.md §4 already notes Rust can be one supervised process with
 tasks per radio. The failure mode you care about is one wedged BlueZ connection
 or one panic, not MMU isolation between Polar and EMAY. Separate processes:

 - Multiply reconnect policy implementations (or require a shared crate anyway),
 - Do not protect against bluetoothd death,
 - Do not protect against vitalsd death (shared fate for detection).

 Prefer one collector supervisor + one vitalsd + one cns-engine + one klaxond if
 Rust wins. Keep process isolation at the life-safety boundary (detection/alarm vs
 ingest), not per sensor. Taste/architecture; not a silent wrong-number bug.

 ### Staging drain concurrency — the migration disagrees with the amendment, and
 both are incomplete

 Amendment §2.2: CREATE TEMP TABLE staging … ON COMMIT DROP (session-private,
 correct).
 db/deploy/samples.sql: permanent UNLOGGED TABLE samples_staging (shared).

 On a shared UNLOGGED table:

 - Two drainers interleave rows; ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING may hide the bug while row
   counts look fine.
 - A failed transaction can leave rows visible to the next session unless you
   TRUNCATE in the same transaction before COPY.
 - No pg_advisory_lock / flock on the spool is specified.

 Single systemd unit does not save you from “I ran the binary by hand during an
 incident.” Wrong as checked in. Fix: TEMP table per session or UNLOGGED +
 TRUNCATE + advisory lock in the same transaction; document one-drainer invariant
 with a lock file.

 ### Append-only vs retention — direct contradiction

 Design §2.5 / amendment §5: no DELETE; tombstones.
 Amendment §3.4 / db/deploy/retention.sql: add_retention_policy('samples',
 INTERVAL '2 years') drops chunks.

 That is a hard delete of raw history. Continuous aggregates surviving does not
 make the append-only claim true. Pick one story:

 - “Raw is disposable after rollup; append-only applies inside the retention
   window,” or
 - “Raw is forever; no retention policy.”

 Right now the law says both. Implementers will follow Timescale and the docs will
 lie during the next incident review.
