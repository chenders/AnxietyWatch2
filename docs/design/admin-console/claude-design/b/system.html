Direction B — Preflight

Every assertion is a line item that is signed, open, or visibly unsigned.

Rationale

A pre-flight checklist solves a problem that looks like this one. A person is responsible for a machine they cannot fully see, the failure modes are quiet, and the acceptable answer is not "it seemed fine" but "these forty-one things were checked, by name, and here is who checked each one and when." The checklist's power is that an unchecked item is structurally impossible to confuse with a passed one, because a passed item has a mark in the box and an unchecked item has an empty box.

That gives this direction its answer to the three-state problem, and it works at two ranges at once. Up close, unsigned is an empty signature box — the absence of a mark, which cannot be mistaken for a mark in greyscale, in a photograph, or under any colour-vision condition. From across a room, that box is periwinkle, a hue used for nothing else in the system. Four dispositions, four hues, four stamp shapes: the colour gets you to the right row in a second, and the shape is what still tells you the truth when the colour is gone.

The layout follows from the same idea. There is no dashboard grid: the sheet is a document, read top to bottom, ordered by what each item costs the record. It opens with a sentence rather than a light — "Not signed off. Three items are unsigned and one is open" — because that is the output of a checklist, and it is legible from bed in about a second.

Why signed gets a colour. A sheet where only faults are coloured tells you what is wrong but not that anything was checked, and this product's claim is the second thing. Teal #5FD3C4 makes "thirty-three of forty-one items were signed by a named prober" a visible fact rather than an arithmetic one. It is deliberately blue-shifted: a true green sits on top of amber under deuteranopia, and teal does not.

Grey is now reserved for exactly two things: ordinary text, and an item struck as not applicable. Struck is the only state in the system with nothing to assert, so it is the only one that stays colourless — which also means a grey row on this sheet always means "this does not apply to me", never "we have not looked".

Five vocabularies

Each has its own shape. A component may show two at once; it never merges them.

1 · Health assertion — can this be relied on right now?

Three values, carried by the stamp. Never appears without a freshness reading and a signer.

Current confidence and last known outcome are separate facts and both stay on screen. Losing sight of something does not erase what was last seen, and the reason is the only part that can be acted on.

2 · Lifecycle — what happened to a piece of work?

A left bar and a state word. Never a stamp, because a run is not an assertion about the machine's health.

Queued · position 2 Running · 04:12 elapsed Succeeded · 06:41 Failed · rate limit Cancelled by operator

A finished upload is not signed. A queued job is not unsigned. A failed run becomes an unsigned item only if the thing it should have produced is now stale.

3 · Process state — systemd's own words

Reported, never interpreted.

active (running) activating (start) failed (Result: exit-code) inactive (dead)
active (running)  + Unsigned  — the collector is alive, reports no error, and has written nothing for nine hours. Both facts are true. Collapsing them into one status is how this failure stays invisible.

4 · Operator suppression — a deferral

Expected silence is deferred with an expiry and a named signer. A deferral suppresses the report, never the attempts, and it is never re-badged as configuration.

Deferred to 09 Aug · operator Deferred 6 more nights · operator

5 · Configuration — struck from the sheet

Something not owned, or declared out of use. Struck, with a reason and a date. Removed from the count and from the report; still visible, so the sheet stays honest.

— Struck Struck 02 June 2026 — no BP cuff owned.

The distinction that protects the third state. A configured ring that went unworn is a deferred unsigned item with an expiry. A device that does not exist is struck. If every optional thing renders as an empty box, the empty box becomes wallpaper and the sheet stops working.

Confirmations, proportionate to consequence

Three weights. Reversible actions confirm inline. A recoverable cost gets a plain dialog. Anything unrecoverable lists what will be lost and requires the object's name to be typed.
