1b DIRECTION B

Penumbra

An almost-invisible object. Left alone at 3 a.m. the panel is black except for a single hairline of light and one word — about 1% of the field emitting, dim enough to sit on a nightstand without registering as a screen. Everything else is withheld until something changes or a hand arrives. The bet is that a bedside instrument should behave like a room does: quiet by default, and unmistakable when it isn't.

The resolution of the tension is geometry instead of luminance. One horizontal line carries the tier: its thickness, continuity and segmentation change, and those are readable at three feet from the corner of a half-open eye at a contrast ratio well under what text needs. Escalation makes the line thicker and longer, not more saturated — the whole system spans 3 px to 44 px of lit height, and only the final tier is allowed to move. Absence has its own geometry: the line breaks. A gap is the one thing that cannot be mistaken for calm, because calm here is literally an unbroken line.

WHY IT CANNOT READ AS FINE At rest, “fine” is one unbroken hairline in a warm-neutral olive at minimum emission. “Can’t assess” is a broken line in cool slate at higher emission, with a question mark floating above the break, the word CAN’T ASSESS where CLEAR was, and a dashed hollow mark instead of a solid one. Four independent channels — continuity, hue temperature, emission, and word — all change at once. Grayscale luminance separation between the two: 2.2:1.
Absence does not escalate to an alarm and does not stay silent: it is the one state that brightens the resting panel without sounding anything. The line is allowed to wake itself to report that it can no longer see.

RESTING VS AWAKE

Resting is the default and the design’s whole premise: one 3 px line, one 46px word, nothing else — roughly 1% of the field lit. Touch fades the rest in over 140 ms, holds it 20 seconds after the last contact, then fades back over 600 ms so nothing snaps in a dark room. A tier change wakes the panel instantly with no fade at all: the abruptness is the signal. Nothing dims while an alarm is active.
