# Aircraft noise watch — status, 2026-08-13

Written after the enclosure/fan rebuild. Supersedes nothing; `ADSB-HANDOFF.md`
remains the design record, with its §5 corrections still applying.

---

## Where we were

At the start of this work the system was a single daemon, `panel-adsb-status`,
reading dump1090 and drawing a radar on the bedside clinical panel. It could not
identify the aircraft that actually generate the complaints here, it kept no
record of what flew over, and the two open requests from the handoff — a Hue
warning light and an FR24 integration — were unbuilt.

Three findings reshaped the design:

- **~34 seconds is the entire warning budget** for a Life Flight departure off
  the Providence pad, measured from FR24's first fix to closest approach. It is
  set by the pad being 1.6 km away, not by poll rate. Nothing can improve it.
- **FR24 withholds identity for exactly the aircraft that matter.** `reg`, `hex`
  and `callsign` all come back null for the Portland Life Flight tails on the
  summary and registration endpoints, so the obvious cheap design — a
  registration-filtered poll — silently misses the target fleet.
- **The Mode S address is the only reliable identifier.** It is broadcast whether
  or not anything else is, which is what makes local, free identification
  possible at all.

### The Hue chain, verified 2026-08-13

Injected a synthetic `LOUD` aircraft 30 s out; the daemon fired within a second
and logged `warned — N776LF LOUD in 29s`. Both ceiling bulbs flashed cyan and
restored to exactly `on, bri 254, ct 231`. Owner confirmed visually.

Configuration: bedroom ceiling pair (lights 2 and 8), fires on `LOUD` and above
plus any unscored aircraft directly overhead, within 45 s of closest approach,
17 s flash at brightness 200, 10-minute cooldown. Cyan; never red, which belongs
to the medical alarm.

## Half working

**Type coverage.** 32 of 396 airframes carry an exact ICAO type. The enricher
fills these in over time as aircraft recur, and 3 addresses are confirmed absent
from the public database. Everything else falls back to the broadcast emitter
category, which cannot distinguish a 737 MAX from an E175.

**Loudness estimates remain guesses.** The four source levels are unvalidated,
and nothing in the system has ever heard an aeroplane. Exact types now choose the
bucket correctly — a 767 no longer shares a source level with a 737 — but the
buckets themselves are invented. 167 of 709 passes are unscored, which is
reported honestly as UNKNOWN rather than as quiet.

**The receiver has never been observed hearing a Life Flight helicopter.** All
three logged Life Flight passes came from FR24. Every sampled track point is
`source: ADSB`, so the dongle should be able to hear them, and it demonstrably
hears other low local traffic — but until one of the six roster hexes appears in
`aircraft.json`, FR24 remains the only guaranteed path.

## Not working

**UPS fuel gauge (I2C).** SDA is clamped low. Survived a reboot, a full power
cycle, 16 SCL recovery pulses, and a complete disassembly and rebuild. Not a
configuration problem — `dtparam=i2c_arm=on` is correctly set and `/dev/i2c-1`
exists. This is hardware or wiring.

Consequences: no battery percentage, and no automatic safe shutdown on low
battery. The batteries themselves are fine.

**No low-battery shutdown.** During a long outage the Pi will hard-cut mid-write
with PostgreSQL live on that disk. This does not need I2C: GPIO 6 reports AC
loss and works, so a watchdog can shut down cleanly on a timer. Not yet built,
and it is the highest-value remaining item.

## Bugs found and fixed

Recorded because each was found by testing rather than by reading:

- **Hue restore left the wrong latent colour.** Restoring only `{"on": false}`
  looked correct — the light was off, as found — but the bridge kept the flash
  colour as that light's stored state, so the next manual switch-on would have
  come up cyan. Invisible at the time, surfacing hours later.
- **Drain rejected every receiver-learned identity.** Correcting the drain to
  record real provenance made it write `source='adsb'`, which the CHECK did not
  permit. It failed on the first row and stayed dead. The spool absorbed the
  whole backlog — 709 rows, nothing lost — which is the argument for the spool
  in one sentence.
- **Emitter category written into `icao_type`.** `A3` is a size class, not a
  designator. Two vocabularies in one column; 11 rows repaired and the fallback
  removed.
- **`bounds_for` clipped the watch circle.** Rounding to nearest at the API's
  3-decimal limit shaved ~55 m off each edge. Now rounds outward.
- **403 from FR24 was not an auth failure.** urllib's default User-Agent is
  rejected at the edge with an HTML body while curl gets 200.
- **Kiosk mode was cached at page load**, so a maximised window stayed in
  appliance layout permanently.

## Known hazards

**`git pull` in `/home/chris/anxietywatch-panel` is destructive.** The deployed
`index.html` contains ~211 lines of radar UI that exist nowhere in git. Pulling
reverts it. Unchanged from handoff §1 and still unfixed.

**Sqitch is behind by design.** `samples_hypertable` cannot deploy: `samples` is
keyed on `(source, type, session_id, seq)` per amendment 04, which deliberately
excludes `timestamp`, and `create_hypertable` requires the partition column in
every unique index. A real conflict on the medical path, not mine to resolve, so
the aircraft changes were applied directly.

**Retention exceeds FR24's terms by owner decision.** Six months against their
30 days, confined to one constant and documented at the top of
`panel_noise_log.py`.

## Next, in order of value

1. **Low-battery shutdown watchdog** on GPIO 6. No I2C needed. Protects the
   database during a real outage.
2. **Reseat or replace the X1202** to recover the fuel gauge. Cosmetic next to
   item 1.
3. **Watch for a roster hex in the local feed**, which would prove the receiver
   can see Life Flight and demote FR24 to redundancy.
4. **Certificated noise data** (ICAO Annex 16 / FAA AC36-1H publish EPNdB per
   type) to replace the invented source levels. The only path to a loudness
   figure that is not a guess, short of the microphone.
