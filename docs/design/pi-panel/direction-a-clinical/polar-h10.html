Heart rate

Beats per minute, over time

This is the pulse rate the H10 reports. Stress or anxiety can coincide with a rise, but so can movement, exercise, caffeine, illness, medication, and many ordinary situations. A rise or fall is an observation, not a diagnosis or proof of anxiety.

Reference and colors: At rest, 60–100 bpm is the common adult reference from the American Heart Association (teal). Outside that resting reference is amber and needs context; it is not automatically dangerous. Waiting for a current HR reference.

Beat timing

RR interval in seconds

RR

How to read it: each point is the time from one beat to the next. It naturally moves around. Stress or anxiety may coincide with changed beat timing, but breathing, posture, movement, sleep, caffeine, illness, medication, and sensor contact can do the same. Spikes often mean movement, contact changes, or a missed/invalid beat—not automatically a body problem.

Reference and colors: RR has no clinical “okay” range by itself. Blue means the interval passed AnxietyWatch2’s registry-defined 0.2–3.0 s signal-quality filter; red means unusable/out of bounds. This is an engineering filter, not a danger threshold. Waiting for an interval.

Heart-rate variability

Both measures calculated from the same rolling 60-second window

NO UNIVERSAL CUTOFF

Why this is noteworthy: repeated HRV measurements taken under comparable conditions can help reveal a sustained departure from your usual baseline, movement back toward recovery, or a new departure after another stressor. That makes HRV useful for observing patterns in context—not for proving that anxiety caused them or for declaring someone safe or unsafe.

RMSSD · milliseconds

What it means: RMSSD emphasizes short-term beat-to-beat variation. Repeated measurements under similar conditions can be useful for noticing a person’s stress load, recovery toward baseline, or a new departure before recovery is complete. Stress or anxiety may coincide with a change, but breathing, posture, movement, sleep, caffeine, illness, medication, fitness, and sensor contact can also change it. There is no universal healthy or dangerous cutoff; it is not proof of cause or an anxiety detector.

Reference and colors: No universal healthy or dangerous RMSSD cutoff is applied. The amber line is neutral: compare repeated, similar-condition windows rather than treating higher or lower as automatically good or bad.

SDNN · milliseconds

What it means: SDNN describes overall spread in the RR intervals. Repeated measurements under similar conditions can be useful for noticing a person’s stress load, recovery toward baseline, or a new departure before recovery is complete, but SDNN is especially dependent on recording length. Stress or anxiety may coincide with a change, but breathing, posture, movement, sleep, caffeine, illness, medication, fitness, and sensor contact can also change it. There is no universal healthy or dangerous cutoff; it is not proof of cause or an anxiety detector.

Reference and colors: No universal healthy or dangerous SDNN cutoff is applied. The amber line is neutral: compare repeated, similar-condition windows rather than treating higher or lower as automatically good or bad.

What RR timing can tell us

  • Whether beat-to-beat timing is arriving from the strap.
  • With enough valid intervals, time-domain HRV measures such as RMSSD and SDNN can be calculated.
  • Whether the stream has gaps, stale data, or implausible intervals that should be rejected.
Observed now: No live observation.
Derived later: HRV needs a window of many valid beats; it is not inferred from one RR value.

About stress and recovery: HRV is a useful personal trend signal, not a direct anxiety meter. A sustained departure from a person’s comparable baseline, movement back toward baseline, or another departure after a new stressor can be worth noticing alongside how they feel. Breathing, posture, movement, sleep, caffeine, illness, medication, fitness, and sensor contact can all create similar changes, so the graph cannot prove that anxiety caused one.

What this page does not claim

  • It does not measure oxygen saturation or breathing.
  • It does not measure blood pressure.
  • It does not diagnose a condition or determine whether someone is safe.
  • This display is not an alarm and cannot wake anyone.

How to use the colors

Colors describe the reference or signal-quality rule printed inside each graph panel. They are not a diagnosis, an anxiety detector, or an emergency alarm. No red physiologic-danger threshold is applied to HRV or RR because the authoritative sources do not define one for this H10-only view.

Sources: American Heart Association · Target Heart Rates; Task Force HRV standards, Circulation (1996); Nunan et al., systematic review of normal short-term HRV values.
